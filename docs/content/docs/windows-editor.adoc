= Windows Editor

Where we tweak how the windows look: open a window, drag its parts, save, `/reload`.

== Start it

[source,bash]
----
tools/editor.py              # needs PySide6, Pillow and lua5.1
tools/install-editor.sh      # optional: adds it to your desktop's menu
----

== The loop

++++
{{< mermaid >}}
flowchart LR
    game["🎮 Live client<br/>the test realm's"] -- "what each window shows" --> editor["🖱️ Editor<br/>pick, drag, size"]
    editor -- "Ctrl+S" --> files["📄 windows/*.qml<br/>settings/positions.lua"]
    files -- "/reload" --> game
{{< /mermaid >}}
++++

. *In the live client* (the test realm's, below): `/dui layout all`, then `/reload`. It opens
  every window that needs no NPC; `.ui all` brings the NPCs' (mail, auction house, bank, vendor,
  trainer), their tabs turned by themselves. It reports in a chat tab named Layout. The scan is
  the live client's alone (`tools/realm/scan.lua`, from a `.toc` of its own): the addon players
  get has no such command and no scan to load.
. *In the editor:* pick a window at the top, a tab under it, and change what you want. It reads a
  new scan by itself, and a window file changed outside it. *Still to scan* on the right says what's missing.
. *Ctrl+S*, then `/reload` in game.

Rescan when a window looks out of date in the editor: its art and texts come from the last scan.

== Change a part

|===
| To | Do

| Pick | Click it, click again for the one under it. Or the list on the left, Ctrl+F to find. Right-click lists all that's there
| Move | Drag it, or arrows (Shift: 10). It snaps to other parts, Alt stops that
| Size | Gold handles, the *Width* and *Height* fields, or Ctrl + arrows. A part under another grows up into it: what's above shrinks and the gap stays. Sizing a panel sizes what it's stretched on
| Place it in another part | Drag one of its dots onto that part's point. It stays where it is, and moves with that part from then on
| Stretch it | *Stretch left to right* or *top to bottom* in the panel: its sides stay on what it's in
| Undo | Ctrl+Z, Ctrl+Shift+Z. *Reset* forgets what's not saved of a part
| See better | Wheel to zoom, drag the empty space to pan, F to fit. *Show* turns frames, texts, art and outlines on and off
|===

The list on the left is the window's tree: each part is under what it's in, one next to the part
it's placed on sits beside it. Moving a part moves what's placed on it. The *In ...* button in the
panel picks what it's placed on.

A part you change gets a pin (◆) and stays as you set it. Without one it follows the next scan.
*Unpin* gives it back to the scan. Leave unpinned what the game moves by itself.

== Rows of buttons

Pick a button in a row: the panel shows the row. Dragging moves the whole row up or down, its
gold ends change its room.

|===
| Fit | Its buttons

| `fill` | As wide as their texts, the room left shared
| `equal` | All as wide, filling the room
| `center` | As they are, together in the middle
| `ends` | As they are, first and last at the ends
|===

== The foot

A window's buttons and money sit in its foot, so moving the foot moves them. A row of buttons
goes 7 over its bottom and 14 from its sides (`foot_bottom`, `foot_side`). It's 35 high: `footer_height` in *Components*, or
`footer` in that window's settings in `settings/blizzard.lua` for a taller one.

== The rules

*Rules* in the top bar counts what breaks one in the tab shown; press it to mark them on the
window, hover a mark to read it. The rules, from *Components'* `Window`:

* Nothing past the iron: everything 23 in from the window's edge (a panel out beside it aside).
* Nothing over anything: no two buttons or texts over each other.
* No text cut: no line longer than its box.
* The foot: its row of buttons 7 over its bottom, 14 from its sides, 22 high, 6 apart.
* No empty band: a band with nothing in it goes (`band = false` in `settings/blizzard.lua`).
* Its kind's (below).

`tools/check.py` runs them on every window and tab, offscreen, and exits 1 if anything's
broken; `--marked` also draws each tab that breaks one into `tools/pictures/marked`.

== The kinds

Every window is one of four, its `kind` in its file:

[cols="1,3,3"]
|===
| Kind | What it is | Windows

| `list` | a list to pick from on slate, what's picked read on parchment beside or under it
| quest log, trainer, professions, friends, mail, dungeon finder, raid browser, PvP, battlemaster,
key bindings, macros, auction house, bulletin board

| `grid` | things in slots on the stone, the money in the foot
| merchant, bank, guild bank, bags, trade

| `book` | pages behind the banners hanging under it
| character, inspect, spellbook, talents, achievements

| `sheet` | one thing to read or handle
| a letter, socketing, dressing room, copy chat
|===

A new window picks its kind first; the rest follows from it.

== The pictures

`tools/check.py --pictures` draws every tab into `tools/pictures/new`. Once there are kept ones,
it compares: those that changed go side by side, before and after, into
`tools/pictures/changed.png`. `tools/check.py --keep` keeps the new ones. Draw them before a
change and after it: a change to *Components* can't quietly move another window.

== The test realm

A server of our own on this PC, the core ChromieCraft runs (AzerothCore), with playerbots and
its Lua engine, and a live client of the game that runs headless. No NPC to walk to, no dungeon
to queue for: `.ui all` in its chat opens every NPC's window in turn, from the server. Every
check runs there: the scans, the pictures of the real windows, performance; nothing is asked of
the game you play.

[source,bash]
----
tools/realm/up.sh        # in your own terminal: builds it the first time (an hour), starts it
tools/realm/up.sh down   # stops it
----

Account `DUI`, password `DUI` (a game master). Its files are in `~/Projects/diabolic-realm`: the
live client links to the game's files with its own `WTF` (realm 127.0.0.1) and its own
`Interface`: your addons linked, DiabolicUI with a `.toc` that adds the scan. The game you play
isn't touched. `tools/realm/remote.py` drives the test client (start, keys, text, clicks, the
screen) on 127.0.0.1:7999. `tools/check.py --scan` reads the test client's scan:

[source,bash]
----
tools/check.py --scan ~/Projects/diabolic-realm/client/WTF/Account/DUI/SavedVariables/DiabolicUI.lua
----

== Every window at once

*Components* in the top bar, or *Styled as...* on a part: buttons, scroll bars, dropdowns, the
window itself. Press a control in the sample window and change its values: it shows at once.
Save and `/reload`. The windows show it in the editor after the live client scans them again.

== By hand

Each window is a file, `windows/<window>.qml`, with each part inside what it's placed on. A part
next to it (coins, a list's next line, a value after its label) sits beside it instead, on its
`id`, and the game still places it. `wowParent` only shows when it's not the block it's in:

[source,qml]
----
Button {
    name: "MacroDeleteButton"
    text: "Delete"
    pin: "place"
    anchors.left: parent.left
    anchors.leftMargin: 14
    anchors.bottom: parent.bottom
    anchors.bottomMargin: 7
    width: 80
    height: 22
}
----

Then run `lua5.1 tools/positions.lua`. It says what it can't read, and writes
`settings/positions.lua` for the game: don't change that one. Plain values only, anchors on
`parent` or an `id`. `tools/lint-qml.sh` checks the files with Qt 6's `qmllint`. A comment over a part stays. A `Ghost` stands for a texture something hangs
on (the trainer's "All" on its tab): it's in the part it belongs to, and moves with it.

== What it can't show

It's close to the game, not the game, so check the result in game:

* Rows cut at a list's edge are drawn whole, or not at all.
* Text can wrap a little differently, and its icons aren't drawn.
* 3D models are only boxes.
* What's in a bag and a long list's other rows are drawn but can't be picked.
* A list made shorter drops its rows only in the editor, except the trainer's: in game, other lists
  keep their count until their module fits them, as `modules/blizzard/trainer.lua` does.

== Changing the editor itself

`tools/editor.qml` is the editor, `tools/editor.py` its backend, `tools/positions.lua` the tool
behind Save. Rows are worked out twice: `windows/WowRow.qml` shows them, `ResolveRow` in
`tools/positions.lua` writes them, so change both. The tests don't load the QML: after changing
it, open a few windows and try it.
